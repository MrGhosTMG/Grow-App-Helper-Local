Topping is a method of growing cannabis in which the grower cuts off the top of the main stem, thereby splitting the top into two parts.
This is a great way to get productive bushes with many colas and branches, as well as to form a crown,
control the growth of the plant, make it lush, healthy and stocky.
In its natural environment, without the intervention of a gardener, cannabis grows in the form of a Christmas tree - it
has a single stem, is lanky and relatively thin in structure. When a grower divides the stem, the cannabis plant begins
to form several colas, focuses on its own development, and becomes wider.
The top splitting is stressful for cannabis, it begins to urgently recover and produces more energy, while focusing on
several colas at once. It is important that during this period marijuana receives enough light for its development.
The plant is pruned at the vegetative stage of development, making a cut below the base of the top, between two nodes.
Note that the stem should not be cut, but the top should be cut off completely so that only the branch on which it was
attached remains. When the plant recovers, the process can be repeated.
Please note that slower growth of cannabis
after topping is a normal response to stress. Trimming is done only after the cannabis plant
has at least 4-5 nodes. If the grower rushes and tops the plant before the specified time, the plant
may die because the young shoots do not have enough energy to recover.
Topping is also allowed at a late stage of vegetation, but since the main stem of cannabis will be longer, it will be
more difficult to form the crown. The technique is never used if inflorescences are being formed or the cannabis has
begun to bloom. In this case, it is necessary to select more gentle training options, such as LST.
During the flowering stage, the plant does not spend its energy on growing in height, because it is focused on
producing inflorescences. Accordingly, topping in this case will not be effective, moreover, the damage caused to the
stem will result in a significant decrease in the productivity of the hemp for the gardener, or he will
lose the harvest altogether.
How to understand that hybrids have redirected their energy to the formation of a new cola.
By enlarged nodes - if they have become denser, then the plant has begun to strengthen them, deliver nutrients to this area.
Over time, the most frequently used stems can become so thick that they will resemble a tree trunk in their structure.

Defoliation in the context of plant cultivation, particularly cannabis and bonsai, is a technique that involves removing leaves
to improve light and ventilation, which can increase the yield and health of the plant.
The main purpose of defoliation is to redirect the plant's energy to the development of larger, more productive top buds,
as well as to improve air circulation, which reduces the risk of mold and fungal diseases.
Defoliation can be applied at different stages of plant growth. In the case of cannabis, leaves are removed as the plant grows,
starting with the lower nodes. Defoliation should be done carefully, removing a small number of leaves at a time, to avoid damaging the plant,
so that the plant has time to grow new leaves.
After defoliation, it is recommended to place the plant in a shaded place for some time.
